# Community model

Decisions made by the Principal on 2026-10-15. They extend "default to
generosity" in [values-and-goals.md](values-and-goals.md) (goal 4). The
prior art behind them is in
[research/mutual-arrangements.md](research/mutual-arrangements.md) and
[research/commons-governance.md](research/commons-governance.md).

## Which kind of economy

Of the modes Mauss and Graeber describe, Casper designs in favour of two:

- **Reciprocity (gift exchange).** Giving, receiving and giving back,
  never quite settled, which is what keeps people bound to each other.
- **Baseline communism.** "From each according to ability, to each
  according to need", the way friends lend a ladder: no accounts kept.

Casper doesn't prevent **barter** (balanced exchange) or **charity**
(one-way giving, often to people you don't know). They're possible; they
aren't what we feature or build defaults around.

## What is visible

- **Giving is visible, owing never is.** What someone gives (space held for
  others, copies kept, recovery shares held, people brought in) is visible
  to their community and works as standing within it. Who is "behind" is
  never shown, computed for display, or hinted at.
- **Gratitude and acknowledgement may be visible too**, but only as genuine
  appreciation: not trophies, not perfunctory, not performative. How thanks
  is presented in public is delicate and still to be designed; see
  [research/commons-governance.md](research/commons-governance.md), part 1.

## Ostrom as the guide

Elinor Ostrom's design principles for long-enduring commons guide us. The
ambition is a facility for **self-organized, self-regulating communities
that share resources**: groups that set their own boundaries and rules,
watch over their own commons, and settle their own disputes, with Casper
providing the means rather than the rules.

## Groups and pairs

- Both matter. **A pair is the degenerate case of a group**: a group of
  two, not a separate concept.
- **Membership changes follow the group's own governance.** Some groups are
  run by one organizer, whose intent alone changes membership. Others are
  run by several people, and a quorum of them must agree.
- Joining a group never commits a member's resources. Each member's
  offering stays their own intent item ([gates.md](gates.md)).

## Replication targets, not destinations

People state a simple intent ("keep 2 copies, on different networks") and
Casper manages the ongoing details quietly: choosing holders, replacing one
that leaves or goes quiet, and speaking up only when the intent can't be
kept.

## Times of need are the opportunity

When someone is short (of space, of copies, of recovery holders, or after
losing a Mac), that is the moment Casper helps them enlist volunteer
contributors and form new bonds of trust, not a failure to hide. Need is
how the gift cycle starts.

> "The cycling gift system is the society."
> (Mary Douglas, foreword to Mauss's *The Gift*, Routledge 1990)

## Naming

- **Good words:** Pact, Circle, Keepers, Swap, Safekeeping.
- **Ghost-themed names are out for this subject.** The Casper "friendly
  ghost" brand is about sociable agents interacting with each other and
  with people. Arrangements between people get plain human words.
