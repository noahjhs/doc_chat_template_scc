How it works

Set it up once, in a conversation. Then forget about it.

01

Point your agent

Your AI agent — or Casper's own guide — installs Casper and asks a couple of questions. You never touch a setting.

02

Choose your people

A friend sends you an invite, or you ask one. They give space; you keep the keys.

03

Confirm, and it's plumbing

You approve once, in Casper's own dialog. From then on every change is mirrored within seconds.

Trust

Your friends hold your files. They can never read them.

Encrypted before it leaves

Names, folders and contents are sealed on your Mac. Not your friends, not us — no one else can open them.

You decide, not your agent

Anything consequential waits for your yes, in a dialog your agent can't click.

Undo anything

Every version for a week, then daily for a month. Ask for yesterday's file and it comes back.

When a computer sleeps

Many hands, so no one has to be always on.

More than one mirror

Friends' computers sleep at different times. With two mirrors, one is usually awake — and each catches up the moment it wakes.

Catchers

A friend with an always-on machine holds just your newest changes until a mirror wakes — usually only megabytes.

Honest status

Ask "are my files safe?" and get a straight answer — down to which recent files aren't protected yet.

An emergent support system

Every bit of space you give makes everyone around you a little safer.

Casper is building a trusted commons: people looking after each other's computers — and, soon, each other's agents. Mirroring is the first thing friends can share. Give space, and be known for it. Default to generosity.
